### iranica-ali-life-2026-10-23
- **Source:** [ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭāleb, i. Life](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ali-b-abi-taleb/ali-b-abi-taleb-i-life/) — Ismail K. Poonawala (section i; Encyclopaedia Iranica headword also credits Etan Kohlberg for section ii).
- **Stable locator / scope read:** Section i. Life; print edition Encyclopaedia Iranica I/8 (1985), pp. 838–848. Consulted search-indexed text for post-ʿUthmān election, Battle of the Camel, move to Kūfa, Ṣiffīn, arbitration, secession/Nahrawān, arbitration meetings and death; individual event subheadings are stable locators. Read the indexed article text covering Ali's reluctant acceptance after Uthman's murder; opposition moving to Basra and the Battle of the Camel; move of political center to Kufa; armies at Siffin and the Quranic codices/arbitration; disagreement over arbitrators, Kharijite secession and Nahrawan; meetings at Dumat al-Jandal and Adhruh; Egypt and loss of territories; death in Kufa.
- **Finding:** The article synthesizes reports from early Arabic histories and emphasizes that accounts of Ali's conflicts were shaped by polemical sectarian historiography. It gives a sequence of events, but often reports competing versions and does not establish exact local footprints.
- **Cautions:** Modern reference synthesis, not a contemporary eyewitness account. Distinguish the author's reconstruction from traditions explicitly attributed to al-Tabari, al-Masudi, al-Baladhuri and others. No exact day for Ali's acceptance is stated; Poonawala says probably several days after Uthman's death. The Battle of the Camel, Kufa and Siffin are regional event labels, not verified map coordinates or city plans.

### iranica-hasan-2026-10-23
- **Source:** [Ḥasan b. ʿAli b. Abi Ṭāleb](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/hasan-b-ali/) — Wilferd Madelung.
- **Stable locator / scope read:** Full headword, especially account from Ali's death through surrender of Iraq and public ceremony; death and burial sections. Print edition Encyclopaedia Iranica XII/1 (2003), pp. 26–28. Read indexed article text on Hasan's support among Kufans after Ali's death; negotiations and proposed terms with Muawiya; surrender in Rabiʿ II 41/August 661; public ceremony at Kufa and return to Medina; accounts of death, poisoning allegations and burial instructions.
- **Finding:** Madelung reports treaty terms and the surrender as transmitted in early sources, while narrating Muawiya's later public repudiation of promises. For Hasan's death, early sources widely report poisoning but disagree on the suspected agent; the article does not present the accusation as a proven fact.
- **Cautions:** Treaty details and speeches are transmitted reports, not independently verified verbatim records. Poisoning is reported by early sources; attribution of responsibility is disputed and must remain qualified. The article's narrative should not be converted into private dialogue or exact staging absent a separate source audit.

- **Source:** [Ḥosayn b. ʿAli, i. Life and Significance in Shiʿism](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/hosayn-b-ali-i/) — Wilferd Madelung.
- **Stable locator / scope read:** Article sections on Yazid's succession, refusal and departure from Medina, Mecca, Kufan correspondence and Muslim b. Aqil, route through Qadisiyya/Thalabiyya/Zubala, Hurr and Karbala, and the confrontation/battle; print edition Encyclopaedia Iranica XII/5 (2004), pp. 493–498. Read indexed article passages covering the succession after Muawiya's death; Husayn's departure to Mecca; letters from Kufa and the mission and execution of Muslim b. Aqil and Hani b. Urwa; departure before full news arrived; road block, news at Thalabiyya and Zubala; encounter with Hurr, blocked routes, Karbala camp, arrival of Umar b. Sa'd's army, negotiations, battle and survivors.
- **Finding:** The article repeatedly distinguishes reported versions and flags disputed proposals attributed to Husayn; it notes that a surviving witness contradicted the claim that Husayn offered submission to Yazid. The route chronology is narrated but does not fix all modern site identifications.
- **Cautions:** The account is a modern synthesis of transmitted reports, not direct eyewitness testimony. Madelung explicitly questions some reports and reports contradictions; do not flatten them into certainty. Road, village and camp sites are not equivalent to precise present-day coordinates. Do not claim all family members died; the article records survivors and captives.

### iranica-karbala-2026-10-23
- **Source:** [Karbala](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/karbala/) — Meir Litvak.
- **Stable locator / scope read:** Headword sections on Karbala as a historical site and the battle; print edition Encyclopaedia Iranica XV/5 (2010), pp. 550–556. Read indexed account of the journey from Medina toward Kufa, interception, arrival on the Karbala plain, army confrontation on 10 Muharram, deaths and captivity; also the later development of Karbala visitation and collective memory.
- **Finding:** The entry gives a concise chronology and clearly separates the battle from the later development of shrine visitation and Shiite collective memory. It describes a traditional 4,000 troop figure and cites Tabari for the narrative.
- **Cautions:** The troop count is a traditional transmitted figure, not a verified census. The entry's retrospective discussion of religious memory is later history, not evidence for battlefield arrangements in 680. The present city and shrine geography do not establish the exact location of the historical camp.

### iranica-asura-2026-10-23
- **Source:** [ʿĀšūrāʾ](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/asura/) — Mahmoud M. Ayoub.
- **Stable locator / scope read:** Section “ʿĀšūrāʾ in Shiʿite piety”; print edition Encyclopaedia Iranica II/8 (1987), pp. 874–876. Read indexed section on the succession after Muawiya's death, Yazid's succession and the opposition of Husayn and Kufa, then the Karbala events and later Ashura commemoration.
- **Finding:** Ayoub frames Ashura as both the 10th of Muharram and a later devotional/memorial observance, distinguishing the event of 680 from the subsequent history of commemorative practice.
- **Cautions:** This is a modern interpretive and religious-history entry, not a primary narrative source for the battle. Its theological interpretation of Husayn's martyrdom is the author's framing; it should be attributed rather than universalized.

### tabari-vol16-egypt-2026-10-23
- **Source:** [The History of al-Ṭabarī, vol. XVI: The Community Divided](https://fliphtml5.com/qzpe/qric/History_of_Al_Tabari_-_Vol_16/) — Al-Ṭabarī; English translation and annotation by Adrian Brockett; SUNY Press, 1997.
- **Stable locator / scope read:** The Events of the Year 36, subsection “ʿAli b. Abi Talib Sends Qays b. Saʿd b. ʿUbada to Be Ruler of Egypt”; print pp. 175–187, especially pp. 181–187 / Kharbita and Muhammad b. Abi Bakr governorship. Online text lines 13008–13831 and 13305–13354, accessed through FlipHTML5 text view. Read the volume's editorial description and the year-36 account of Qays b. Saʿd's appointment, Kharbita opposition, reported security/truce and tax collection, later transfer of Egypt to Muhammad b. Abi Bakr, including the text's conflicting chronology notices.
- **Finding:** The translated compilation presents multiple attributed reports: Kharbita opposition is described as tied to the Uthman killing and Qays is said to make a truce and collect tax without resistance; accounts differ in the sequence of Qays, al-Ashtar and Muhammad b. Abi Bakr.
- **Cautions:** This is a translated medieval chronicle compilation with multiple report chains and internal divergences, not a single uncontested account. The volume's own preface warns that the section is open to special pleading and represents differing viewpoints. It does not support moving the later 658 fall of Egypt or Muhammad b. Abi Bakr's death to Kharbita. The FlipHTML5 host is a third-party viewer; bibliographic identity and pagination are taken from the reproduced SUNY volume front matter.
