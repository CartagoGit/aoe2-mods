The Temple of Apollo image needs a more careful time note than “archaic, therefore absent in Roman Corinth.” The standing columns belong to the sixth-century BCE temple, but the site remained in use and the Romans substantially altered the urban approach and temple interior. This photograph shows the surviving archaic fabric, not a reconstruction of its first-century CE appearance; it cannot establish which Roman-era alterations were present in Paul's time. Commons' file description discusses those Roman modifications, while the photo itself is dated 2006. [File page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:CorintoScaviTempioApollo.jpg).

## Cross-check notes

- The six source pages and six manifest entries match by filename, author, and license. The image manifest's `downloadUrl` fields point to Commons-served thumbnails rather than full-resolution originals; preserve the linked file page for attribution.
- `pilate-inscription-cast.jpg`: the appropriate person credit is Giovanni Dall'Orto, not the museum or an unidentified cast-maker. Commons records a rights-holder attribution-only grant. The official museum page identifies the object as a plaster cast but I found no separate museum or mold-maker credit instruction there. Commons' photographer grant and any cultural-heritage rules are separate questions.
- For the other five images, retain the named photographer/uploader and exact license. ShareAlike applies to the three CC BY-SA photographs; it does not apply to the aqueduct under CC BY 3.0 or the public-domain Apollo photo.
- Historic structures in the photos are visual references only. The Roman Bath-Gymnasium photograph includes modern reconstruction; the Sardis synagogue is later than the Melito episode; Temple E's Octavia identification is uncertain; the Apollo photograph is not a first-century reconstruction.

## Corinth archaeological and relief controls

Five local research archives are credited to James A. Herbst, Corinth Excavations, American School of Classical Studies at Athens, under [CC BY-SA4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). [Source and licensing](https://dataservices.ascsa.org/excavations/ancient-corinth/digital-corinth/maps-gis-data-and-archaeological-data-for-corinth-and-greece) · [Layer audit](corinth-gis-audit.json). The selected shapefile components were extracted from the broader CorinthGIS archive and repackaged without modifying their contents; the contour archive is retained as downloaded. No derived geometry is yet used as certified first-century terrain. Roman line/polygon layers mix phases, city walls include archaic/classical/late-Roman records, and the modern contour raster lacks declared CRS. These archives cannot be used wholesale as a snapshot of50CE. Share-alike/attribution obligations follow any eventual adaptation; research inclusion does not approve mod distribution.
