# Composed objectives capability profile v2

The campaign and scenario source envelope remains schema version 1. A scenario may opt in to the separately versioned `capabilityProfile` with `profile: "composed-world-ledger"` and `schemaVersion: 2`. This separates source-envelope compatibility from the semantics of the profile and its compiled intermediate representation (IR). Existing v1 sources, fixtures, localization IDs, and string IDs do not change.

The v2 compiler validates the profile and emits a scenario IR marked `schemaVersion: 2` with a distinct `capability` payload. It preserves author-supplied identifiers. It does not lower compound predicates to the older v1 condition list. Legacy v1 scenarios continue to compile as v1.

Native campaign rendering, the candidate/export path, and the direct Python exporter reject any v2 profile with `capability-not-implemented` until the native predicates and their save/load behavior are implemented and certified in i00003/S3. No entrypoint may silently remove the profile or approximate its all-required logic using legacy conditions.

The zod contracts, structural validator, deterministic compiler output, and pure reducer in this slice are technical references for authoring and adversarial unit tests. They are not a claim of native engine support, a substitute for engine QA, or proof that game triggers implement these semantics.

The reference state lives in `packages/gameplay-state`. The snapshot schema validates saved data; the factory creates and normalizes it; ledger helpers preserve unique deliveries; predicate evaluation reads that state; the reducer applies typed observations and transactions. Callers import the factory and reducer directly. There is no barrel or re-export facade. A token comparison verified that splitting the corrected implementation preserved all 34 declaration bodies, excluding export visibility and formatting.

A snapshot contains a fingerprint of its objective/world/phase/reward/roster contract. Loading it against another contract fails explicitly. Arrival history records phase, entity and destination, allowing reception followed by withdrawal without granting a second delivery for the same entity. Exported ledgers retain the actual intersection of recipients. Current survival is evaluated from entity state; past observations cannot revive a destroyed entity. Derived records require collected input identities, and receipt-generated records require their original delivery.

World observations are trusted, typed input to this reference model. A zone visit does not prove control or passability. Continuous control is checked until its declared later objective; a loss terminates the mission when the contract makes it irrecoverable. All terminal entry paths close optional objectives, and defeat takes priority. Ordered departure may refer to a specific non-departure `receiptRequirement` within its own objective; waiting for that objective's own latch without such a receipt is rejected as a cycle.

Inventory ownership (`carriedRecords`) remains unsupported and is rejected by the structural compiler gate. Free resurrection has no action in this profile. A future recovery capability must specify eligible entities, finite replacement identities, costs, receipts and save/load semantics before it can be accepted. The protected recovery account alone does not implement replacement. These limitations remain pending infrastructure work; neither the reference model nor these tests certify the complete historical scenario contracts.

For arrival predicates, `distinctDestinationCount` is a lower bound on receiving zones with qualifying unique arrivals. When `minimumPerDestination` is supplied, every zone assigned by `destinations` must reach that minimum, in addition to `requiredCount`. Deliveries by ineligible identities do not fill either minimum. Structural validation rejects too many requested destinations, undersupplied assignments, or distribution fields attached to a non-arrival state. Regression tests preserve these counts through save/load and forbid replacing one receiving post with extra deliveries at another.

Main entity counters reject duplicate identities, and the reference evaluator defensively deduplicates before counting. Civilian protection is continuously enforced from `activeFrom` until `activeUntil` when a typed observation reports loss; an unknown initial observation cannot satisfy the protection objective. A prior objective latch cannot excuse a later loss during the interval.

Physical-stock predicates (`atHub`, `accountEntityIds`, or `maximumNavigableDistanceTiles`) are rejected by compilation and cannot latch in the reference model. The current accounts contain global amounts, not per-carrier inventories or measured navigable distance. A future stock-ownership/location contract must provide those observations and conservation rules before these predicates can be certified.

Identity lists are sets throughout the contract, including survivor groups, area counters, secondary arrivals and finite waves. Structural checks reject repeats and impossible wave thresholds; reference counters deduplicate defensively. Receipt queries bind their flag to a unique resource transaction, its actual objective and the exact four-resource vector; a smaller or unrelated payment cannot satisfy them. Reading a receipt never debits again. Peaceful profiles may declare an empty military roster, and a legitimate atomic payment may have one recipient: distributing to two recipients remains an explicit scenario decision rather than a universal infrastructure requirement.

Funded derivation optionally declares `requiredPaymentReceipts`, reusing the exact receipt reference fields (`flag`, `fromObjective`, `amounts`, `newDebit:false`). These references must bind to a unique payment in the same objective or a mandatory predecessor. The reducer requires the active objective phase and prerequisites, collected surviving inputs, every declared payment receipt, and undestroyed outputs before recording derivation. It neither charges again nor accepts a partial/unrelated debit. Duplicate derivation remains idempotent through its identity ledger and save/load. Unfunded legacy v2 derivations omit this optional field and retain their collected-input contract; no source migration or string-ID change occurs. The snapshot fingerprint rejects reuse of old saves against changed derivation contracts. These reference semantics still have no native implementation or game certification.

## Optional arrival-after-departure causality (2026-10-13)

An entity-state arrival may declare `arrivalAfterDeparture: {objective, requirement}`. The reference must identify an ordered departure of the same actors in the same objective or a mandatory predecessor. A departure records scoped actor facts only after its receipt and during its active objective. A gated arrival is observed after that exact departure and needs its own requirement fact; an older global arrival or another departure of the same actor cannot satisfy it. Exported recipient ledgers and receipt-generated output entitlements apply the same exact producer rule; receipt-generation dependencies also participate in cycle detection.

Receipt/departure/arrival cycles are rejected structurally. Shared objective ancestry is extracted to `objective-ancestry.helper.ts` and reused by funded production and causal arrival validation. Existing v2 profiles omit the optional guard and retain buffered-arrival behavior; no new snapshot fields or source/snapshot version change is required. Declaring a guard changes the existing contract fingerprint, so a prior save cannot be silently promoted to that new contract. Native observation, routing and all full v2 exports remain unimplemented and rejected. This is a reference capability, not an AoE2 transport proof.
