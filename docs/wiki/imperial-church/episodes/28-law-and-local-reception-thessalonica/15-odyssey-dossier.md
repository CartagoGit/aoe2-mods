## Historical frame and fixed outcome

EN: On 27 February 380 a constitution was issued at Thessalonica in the names of Gratian, Valentinian and Theodosius. Its heading addresses the people of Constantinople. It prescribes the faith associated with Damasus of Rome and Peter of Alexandria and threatens those it classifies as dissenters. A prescription is not evidence of immediate acceptance or uniform enforcement. This reconstruction follows fictional local preparation and handovers; the distant addressee remains outside the map.

ES: El 27 de febrero de 380 se emitió en Tesalónica una constitución en nombre de Graciano, Valentiniano y Teodosio. Su encabezamiento se dirige al pueblo de Constantinopla. Prescribe la fe asociada con Dámaso de Roma y Pedro de Alejandría y amenaza a quienes clasifica como disidentes. Una prescripción no demuestra aceptación inmediata ni cumplimiento uniforme. Esta reconstrucción sigue preparativos y entregas locales ficticios; el destinatario distante permanece fuera del mapa.

EN: Two fictional local products reached different receiving courts, and both original couriers returned on their own recorded handovers. Those local receipts establish neither delivery to Constantinople nor compliance by its inhabitants. The law remains a historical prescription with a named addressee, not a count of converted people. The next episode moves to the council at Constantinople in 381 and the different questions preserved in its texts.
