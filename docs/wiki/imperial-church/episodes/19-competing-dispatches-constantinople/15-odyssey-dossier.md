## Historical frame and fixed outcome

EN: After Constantine’s death in 337, succession brought violence. Sources report soldiers killing Dalmatius; responsibility is disputed, and several reports may share a lost-source tradition. Constantinople is our court context, not a verified killing site. This fictional neighborhood defense protects ordinary households and three local context notes. It cannot save historical victims or decide which heir ordered the killings.

ES: Tras la muerte de Constantino en 337, la sucesión trajo violencia. Las fuentes refieren soldados que mataron a Dalmacio; la responsabilidad se discute y varios relatos pueden compartir una fuente perdida. Constantinopla es nuestro contexto de corte, no lugar acreditado de la muerte. Esta defensa ficticia vecinal protege hogares ordinarios y tres notas locales de contexto. No salva víctimas históricas ni decide qué heredero ordenó las muertes.

EN: Six fictional households reached local safety while two disclosed approaches stayed controlled and passable. Three separate context notes supported one qualified local dispatch. That success neither reversed Dalmatius’s death nor settled responsibility or the imperial partition. This act closes with contested succession. The next act begins with the disputed chronology and divided assembly associated with Sardica.
