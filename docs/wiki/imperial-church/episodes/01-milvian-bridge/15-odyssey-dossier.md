## Historical frame and fixed outcome

EN: Constantine defeated Maxentius near the Tiber in 312; Maxentius died during the retreat. The battle is conventionally dated 28 October, while the displayed Latin of Lactantius 44 has a conflicting day. Lactantius and Eusebius give different Christian accounts of signs and victory; their interpretation is not an objective proof of divine intervention. Our northern-bank tactical pocket uses fictional units and positions, not a reconstructed army deployment.

ES: Constantino derrotó a Majencio cerca del Tíber en 312; Majencio murió durante la retirada. La batalla se fecha convencionalmente el 28 de octubre, mientras el latín consultado de Lactancio 44 contiene un día discrepante. Lactancio y Eusebio ofrecen relatos cristianos distintos de los signos y la victoria; su interpretación no prueba objetivamente una intervención divina. Nuestro sector táctico de la ribera norte usa unidades y posiciones ficticias, no un despliegue militar reconstruido.

EN: The fictional field officer reached a northern rally point while two local positions remained usable. This bounded tactical success does not simulate Maxentius’ drowning or reconstruct the whole battle. Constantine’s victory and later entry into Rome remain the historical frame; the next episode separates the Milan meeting from the later published letter.
